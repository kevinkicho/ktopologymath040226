Conformal Maps

$w = f(z),\quad f'(z)\neq 0$

A holomorphic map with non-zero derivative is conformal: it preserves angles between curves at every point.

$\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\right|=\left|\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\right|,\quad \frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\perp\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}$

Cauchy-Riemann equations ensure the Jacobian is a rotation+dilation — no shear.

The Conformal Group

In $d\geq 3$: finite-dimensional $SO(d+1,1)$ (dilations, translations, SCTs, rotations).

In $d=2$: infinite-dimensional! Every holomorphic function is locally conformal. The symmetry algebra is the Witt algebra with generators $\ell_n = -z^{n+1}\partial_z$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.

$[\ell_m,\ell_n] = (m-n)\ell_{m+n}$

Möbius / SL(2,ℂ)

$w = \frac{az+b}{cz+d},\quad ad-bc=1$

Maps circles and lines to circles and lines. The global conformal group of $\hat{\mathbb{C}}$ is $PSL(2,\mathbb{C})$.

Joukowski Map

$w = z + \frac{1}{z}$

Maps circles to airfoil cross-sections. Used in aerodynamics — the conformal image of a circle is a wing!

Worldsheet Connection

$w = \ln z$ "unrolls" a cylinder to a plane. The closed string worldsheet is conformally equivalent to $\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$ — this is why CFT is the language of string theory.

Operator Product Expansion

$\mathcal{O}_i(z,\bar z)\,\mathcal{O}_j(w,\bar w)\sim\!\sum_k C_{ij}^k\,(z-w)^{h_k-h_i-h_j}\mathcal{O}_k(w)$

The OPE is the fundamental tool of CFT: as two local operators approach each other, their product is captured by an infinite sum over operators in the theory, with coefficients $C_{ij}^k$ (the OPE coefficients) and singular powers controlled by conformal dimensions.

Singularity Structure

When $h_k < h_i+h_j$: the OPE is singular (pole). The leading singularity as $z\to w$:

$\sim(z-w)^{h_k-h_i-h_j}$,  exponent $= h_k-h_i-h_j$

Non-integer exponent ⇒ branch cut. Integer exponent ⇒ pole. $h_k=h_i+h_j$ ⇒ logarithm.

Stress Tensor OPE

$T(z)\,T(w)\sim\dfrac{c/2}{(z-w)^4}+\dfrac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\dfrac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}$

This is the fundamental OPE of CFT. The $c/2$ pole encodes the conformal anomaly. It is equivalent to the Virasoro commutation relations.

T(z)·Primary

$T(z)\,\phi(w)\sim\dfrac{h\,\phi(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\dfrac{\partial\phi(w)}{z-w}$

This OPE defines a primary field of weight $h$. The $1/(z-w)^2$ pole gives the scaling dimension; the $1/(z-w)$ pole gives the derivative (infinitesimal translation).

Radial Quantization

OPEs encode equal-time commutators via the contour integral (contour = small circle around $w$):

Conformal Bootstrap

The four-point function of identical scalars $\langle\mathcal{O}(x_1)\cdots\mathcal{O}(x_4)\rangle$ depends only on cross-ratios $u,v$ via a conformal block decomposition.

$\mathcal{G}(u,v)=\sum_{\Delta,\ell}p_{\Delta,\ell}\,g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v)$

Crossing Symmetry

Expanding in the s-channel (12→34) vs the t-channel (14→23) must give identical answers — this is the crossing equation:

$\sum_{\Delta,\ell}p_{\Delta,\ell}\left[v^{\Delta_\mathcal{O}}g_{\Delta,\ell}(u,v)-u^{\Delta_\mathcal{O}}g_{\Delta,\ell}(v,u)\right]=0$

Conformal Blocks

In 1D the scalar block (for the diagonal $z=\bar z$) is a hypergeometric function:

$g_\Delta(z)=z^\Delta\,{}_2F_1(\Delta,\Delta;2\Delta;z)$

In 2D the full block factorizes: $g_{\Delta,\ell}=k_{h}\,k_{\bar h}$ with $h=(\Delta+\ell)/2$, $\bar h=(\Delta-\ell)/2$ and $k_\beta(z)=z^\beta\,{}_2F_1(\beta,\beta;2\beta;z)$.

Solving the Bootstrap

The semidefinite programming (SDP) approach (Rattazzi-Rychkov-Tonni-Vichi, 2008) turns crossing symmetry into a convex optimization: apply linear functionals $\alpha$ such that $\alpha[F_{\Delta,\ell}]\geq 0$ for all allowed $\Delta,\ell$. This carves out the space of allowed CFT spectra — including exact results for the 3D Ising model ($\Delta_\sigma=0.5181489$).

$\Delta_\sigma = 0.5181489(10)$   (3D Ising)

Minimal Models M(p,q)

$c = 1 - \dfrac{6(p-q)^2}{pq}$

For $p=m$, $q=m+1$ we get the unitary series $c = 1 - \frac{6}{m(m+1)}$ with $m=3,4,5,\ldots$

Kac Determinant

$\det M^{(n)} = \prod_{r,s\geq1,\,rs\leq n} (h-h_{r,s})^{p(n-rs)}$

Vanishing when $h = h_{r,s}$ for some $r,s$ with $rs \leq n$. The Kac table shows the conformal dimensions of all primary fields in the minimal model.

$h_{r,s} = \dfrac{(pr-qs)^2 - (p-q)^2}{4pq}$

Virasoro Algebra

$[L_n,L_m] = (n-m)L_{n+m} + \dfrac{c}{12}n(n^2-1)\delta_{n+m,0}$

The central charge $c$ is the unique central extension of the Witt algebra. For $c < 1$, unitarity forces $c$ to take discrete values — the minimal model series.

Fusion Rules

$\phi_{r_1,s_1} \times \phi_{r_2,s_2} = \sum_{r_3=|r_1-r_2|+1}^{\min(r_1+r_2-1,2p-r_1-r_2-1)} \sum_{s_3=|s_1-s_2|+1}^{\min(s_1+s_2-1,2q-s_1-s_2-1)} \phi_{r_3,s_3}$

Fusion rules are the CFT analog of the Clebsch-Gordan series. They determine which operators appear in the OPE of two primaries.

Liouville Field Theory

$S_L = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}\displaystyle\int\!\bigl(g^{ab}\partial_a\phi\partial_b\phi + 4\pi\mu e^{2b\phi}\bigr)\sqrt{g}\,d^2x$

Liouville theory describes 2D quantum gravity. The field $\phi$ is the conformal factor of the metric: $g_{ab}=e^{2b\phi}\hat g_{ab}$. The exponential potential $e^{2b\phi}$ is the cosmological constant term.

DOZZ Formula

$C(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3) = \dfrac{\Upsilon_0\,\Upsilon(2\alpha_1)\Upsilon(2\alpha_2)\Upsilon(2\alpha_3)}{\Upsilon(\alpha_1+\alpha_2-\alpha_3)\Upsilon(\alpha_2+\alpha_3-\alpha_1)\Upsilon(\alpha_3+\alpha_1-\alpha_2)}$

The Dorn-Otto-Zamolodchikov-Zamolodchikov (DOZZ) formula gives the exact 3-point structure constants for Liouville CFT. $\Upsilon(x)$ is the special function built from Barnes double gamma functions.

Liouville Equation

$\Delta\phi = \mu e^{2b\phi}$

The classical equation of motion. On the sphere, this is the Liouville equation — a nonlinear PDE with exact soliton solutions. The quantum version is exactly solvable via conformal bootstrap.

Conformal Dimensions

$\Delta_\alpha = \alpha(Q-\alpha),\quad Q = b + \frac{1}{b}$

Primary fields $V_\alpha = e^{2\alpha\phi}$ have conformal dimension $\Delta_\alpha$. The spectrum is continuous for $\alpha \in Q/2 + i\mathbb{R}$ (normalizable states).

Wess-Zumino-Witten Model

$S_{WZW} = \dfrac{k}{16\pi}\displaystyle\int\text{Tr}(\partial_\mu g^{-1}\partial^\mu g)\,d^2x + k\Gamma$

The WZW model describes a group-valued field $g(x) \in G$ with a topological term $\Gamma$ (the Wess-Zumino term). For $G=SU(2)$, the level $k$ is a positive integer.

Current Algebra

$J^a(z)J^b(w) \sim \dfrac{k\delta^{ab}}{(z-w)^2} + \dfrac{if^{ab}_c J^c(w)}{z-w}$

The chiral currents $J^a(z)$ generate an affine Lie algebra $\widehat{su}(2)_k$. The level $k$ appears as the central extension — analogous to $c$ in the Virasoro algebra.

Conformal Dimensions

$\Delta_j = \dfrac{j(j+1)}{k+2}$

Primary fields $\Phi_j$ transform in the spin-$j$ representation of $SU(2)$. Their conformal dimension depends on both $j$ and the level $k$.

Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov Equation

$\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z_i} + \dfrac{1}{k+2}\sum_{j\neq i}\dfrac{t^a_i t^a_j}{z_i-z_j}\right)\langle\Phi_1\cdots\Phi_n\rangle = 0$

The KZ equation is a differential equation satisfied by correlation functions in WZW models. It relates the conformal dependence on $z_i$ to the action of the Lie algebra generators $t^a$.

Fusion Rules for SU(2)ₖ

$\phi_{j_1} \times \phi_{j_2} = \sum_{j_3=|j_1-j_2|}^{\min(j_1+j_2,k-j_1-j_2)} \phi_{j_3}$

Fusion is truncated by the level $k$: only representations with $j \leq k/2$ appear, and the sum is cut off at $\min(j_1+j_2, k-j_1-j_2)$.
