π₁ — Fundamental Group

  • $\pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb{Z}$ — loops wind around a circle
  • $\pi_1(S^2)=0$ — sphere is simply connected
  • $\pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ — torus has two generators
  • $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{p\})=\mathbb{Z}$ — punctured plane
  • $\pi_1(F_2)$ — figure-8 gives free group
Van Kampen's theorem: If $X=A\cup B$ with $A\cap B$ path-connected, then $\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(A)*_{\pi_1(A\cap B)}\pi_1(B)$. This allows computing $\pi_1$ by cutting a space into simpler pieces.
Homotopy equivalence: $f,g:X\to Y$ are homotopic if there is a continuous $H:X\times[0,1]\to Y$ with $H(\cdot,0)=f$ and $H(\cdot,1)=g$. Spaces with the same homotopy type share all $\pi_n$.
Deformation retract: A subspace $A\subset X$ is a deformation retract if there is a homotopy $H:X\times I\to X$ with $H(x,0)=x$, $H(x,1)\in A$, $H(a,t)=a$ for $a\in A$.
Orange loops wind around a hole: $[\gamma]\neq e\in\pi_1$. Blue loops are contractible: $[\gamma]=e$.

Universal cover of $S^1$: the real line $\mathbb{R}$, with covering map $p(t)=e^{2\pi it}$. Going around $S^1$ once lifts to moving by 1 on $\mathbb{R}$.

Covering Spaces

  • $p:\tilde{X}\to X$ — covering map, every $x$ has evenly covered nbd
  • $p:\mathbb{R}\to S^1$ — $p(t)=e^{2\pi it}$, fiber $= \mathbb{Z}$
  • $\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ — deck group of universal cover
  • Monodromy: $\pi_1(B)\to\mathrm{Sym}(F)$
  • Galois correspondence — subgroups $\leftrightarrow$ covers
Unique path lifting: Given a path $\gamma$ in the base $B$ starting at $b_0$ and a lift $\tilde{b}_0\in p^{-1}(b_0)$, there is a unique lift $\tilde\gamma$ in $\tilde X$ starting at $\tilde{b}_0$.
Deck transformations are self-homeomorphisms $\tilde X\to\tilde X$ commuting with $p$. For the universal cover they form a group isomorphic to $\pi_1(X)$.
Figure-8 universal cover: an infinite tree — the Cayley graph of the free group $F_2=\langle a,b\rangle$. Each vertex has 4 edges labeled $a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}$.

Higher Homotopy Groups

  • $\pi_n(S^n)=\mathbb{Z}$ — degree of maps $S^n\to S^n$
  • $\pi_1(S^2)=0$ — sphere is simply connected
  • $\pi_2(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ — Hopf's theorem (1931)
  • $\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ — Hopf fibration $\eta$
  • $\pi_3(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}$ — identity map generator
  • $\pi_4(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}/2$ — first non-trivial unstable
Hopf fibration: $h:S^3\to S^2$. Fibres are great circles $S^1\subset S^3$, any two fibres are linked. This is the generator $\eta$ of $\pi_3(S^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, discovered by Hopf in 1931.
Long exact sequence of a fibration $F\hookrightarrow E\to B$:
$\cdots\to\pi_n(F)\to\pi_n(E)\to\pi_n(B)\to\pi_{n-1}(F)\to\cdots$
For Hopf: $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$ gives $\pi_n(S^3)\cong\pi_n(S^2)$ for $n\geq 3$.
Degree: For $f:S^n\to S^n$, $\deg(f)=$ number of preimages of a regular value (with sign). $\deg(\mathrm{id})=1$, $\deg(\mathrm{const})=0$, $\deg(\mathrm{antipodal})=(-1)^{n+1}$.

CW Complexes & Retracts

  • Torus: 1 point + 2 loops + 1 square
  • $S^n$: 1 zero-cell + 1 $n$-cell
  • $\mathbb{R}P^2$: one cell in each dimension 0–2
  • Euler char: $\chi=V-E+F$ (cells)
  • Torus: $\chi=1-2+1=0$ ✓
  • $S^2$: $\chi=1+0+1=2$ ✓
Deformation retract: The Möbius band $M$ deformation retracts onto its central circle $S^1$: move every point radially toward the core. Thus $\pi_1(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}$.
$\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\}\simeq S^{n-1}$: The retraction $r(x)=x/|x|$ gives a deformation retraction via $H(x,t)=x/|x|^t$. So $\pi_k(\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\})\cong\pi_k(S^{n-1})$.
Attaching maps determine the topology. The torus word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ gives abelian $\pi_1$; the Klein bottle $abab^{-1}$ gives non-abelian $\pi_1$.

Vortex in superfluid: order parameter $\psi=|\psi|e^{i\theta}$ winds $n$ times around the core. Classified by $\pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb{Z}$, winding number $n$.

Topological Defects

  • $\pi_1(S^1)=\mathbb{Z}$ → vortex lines (superfluids, nematics)
  • $\pi_1(SO(3))=\mathbb{Z}/2$ → disclinations in crystals
  • $\pi_2(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ → monopoles (hedgehog)
  • $\pi_3(S^3)=\mathbb{Z}$ → Skyrmions (baryons!)
  • $\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$ → Hopf solitons in nematics
't Hooft–Polyakov monopole: A hedgehog $\hat n=\hat r$ at spatial infinity defines a map $S^2\to S^2$ of degree 1. The magnetic charge is quantized: $g=4\pi n/e$ (Dirac condition).
Skyrmion: In nuclear physics (Skyrme 1961), baryons are topological solitons: maps $S^3\to SU(2)\cong S^3$ with degree = baryon number. Topological stability prevents decay.
Vortex quantization: In a BEC/superconductor, circulation $\oint\mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=nh/m$. The winding number $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ is a topological invariant — cannot change without creating/annihilating defects.

Bott periodicity: $\pi_{n+2}(U(\infty))\cong\pi_n(U(\infty))$. Period 2 for unitary groups: $0, \mathbb{Z}, 0, \mathbb{Z}, \ldots$

Bott Periodicity & K-theory

  • $\pi_0(U)=0$ (connected)
  • $\pi_1(U)=\mathbb{Z}$ (winding number)
  • $\pi_2(U)=0$
  • $\pi_3(U)=\mathbb{Z}$ (Skyrmion charge)
  • Period 2: $\pi_{n+2}\cong\pi_n$
Orthogonal period 8: $\pi_*(O(\infty))$ cycles through $\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}/2,0,\mathbb{Z},0,0,0,\mathbb{Z}$ then repeats. This is the famous "8-fold way" in topology.
K-theory: $\tilde{K}(S^n)\cong\pi_{n-1}(U(\infty))$ by Bott periodicity. So $\tilde{K}(S^{\mathrm{even}})\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $\tilde{K}(S^{\mathrm{odd}})=0$ (complex K-theory).
10 symmetry classes: Combining time-reversal $T$, particle-hole $C$, and chiral $S$ symmetries gives 10 Altland-Zirnbauer classes. In each spatial dimension $d$, topological phases are classified by $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/2$, or $0$ — the "periodic table of topological matter."

Simplicial homology: $H_0, H_1, H_2$ of a simplicial complex. Cycles (closed chains) are shown in orange, boundaries in cyan. Euler characteristic $\chi = \sum (-1)^n \text{rank}(H_n)$.

Homology Groups $H_n$

  • $C_n$ — $n$-chains (formal sums of $n$-simplices)
  • $\partial_n: C_n \to C_{n-1}$ — boundary map
  • $Z_n = \ker\partial_n$ — $n$-cycles (closed chains)
  • $B_n = \operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}$ — $n$-boundaries
  • $H_n = Z_n / B_n$ — homology group
  • $\chi = \sum (-1)^n \text{rank}(H_n)$
Simplicial complex: A set of simplices (points, edges, triangles, tetrahedra) closed under taking faces. The boundary of an $n$-simplex is the alternating sum of its $(n-1)$-dimensional faces.
Euler characteristic: For a triangulated surface, $\chi = V - E + F$. By the Euler-Poincaré theorem, $\chi = \sum (-1)^n \text{rank}(H_n)$. For a sphere: $\chi = 2$; for a torus: $\chi = 0$.
Betti numbers: $\beta_n = \text{rank}(H_n)$. $\beta_0$ counts connected components, $\beta_1$ counts independent loops, $\beta_2$ counts voids.

Hurewicz theorem: the first non-trivial homotopy group $\pi_n(X)$ maps isomorphically to $H_n(X)$. For $n=1$, $\pi_1(X)^{\text{ab}} \cong H_1(X)$.

Hurewicz Homomorphism

  • $h: \pi_n(X) \to H_n(X)$ — Hurewicz map
  • $h([f]) = f_*([S^n])$ — push forward fundamental class
  • $\pi_1^{\text{ab}} \cong H_1$ — abelianization
  • $\pi_n \cong H_n$ for first non-trivial $n$
  • $T^2$: $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}^2$, $H_1=\mathbb{Z}^2$
  • $S^2$: $\pi_2=\mathbb{Z}$, $H_2=\mathbb{Z}$
Abelianization: The Hurewicz map $h: \pi_1(X) \to H_1(X)$ sends a homotopy class $[\gamma]$ to its homology class. It factors through the abelianization $\pi_1 / [\pi_1,\pi_1]$, giving an isomorphism $\pi_1^{\text{ab}} \cong H_1$.
Higher Hurewicz: If $X$ is $(n-1)$-connected ($\pi_k(X)=0$ for $k\lt n$), then $h: \pi_n(X) \to H_n(X)$ is an isomorphism for $n\ge 2$. This is the Hurewicz isomorphism theorem.
Examples: Torus: $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}^2$, $H_1=\mathbb{Z}^2$ (abelian already). Wedge of circles: $\pi_1=F_2$, $H_1=\mathbb{Z}^2$ (free group abelianizes to free abelian). $\mathbb{RP}^2$: $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}/2$, $H_1=\mathbb{Z}/2$.

Seifert–van Kampen: $\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(A)*_{\pi_1(A\cap B)}\pi_1(B)$ when $X=A\cup B$ with $A,B,A\cap B$ path-connected open sets.

Seifert–van Kampen

  • $X=A\cup B$ open cover, $A\cap B$ path-conn.
  • $\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(A)*_{\pi_1(U)}\pi_1(B)$
  • Pushout in Grp — free product with amalgamation
  • $S^1\vee S^1$: $\pi_1=F_2=\langle a,b\rangle$
  • $T^2$: $\langle a,b\mid [a,b]\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$
  • $\mathbb{R}P^2$: $\langle a\mid a^2\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$
Statement: Given open path-connected $A,B\subset X$ with $X=A\cup B$ and $U=A\cap B$ path-connected, the inclusions induce a pushout square in groups, so $\pi_1(X)$ is the amalgamated free product of $\pi_1(A)$ and $\pi_1(B)$ over $\pi_1(U)$.
CW presentation: Attaching an $n$-cell along $f:S^{n-1}\to X$ for $n\ge 2$ kills $[\tilde f]\in\pi_{n-1}$ (relative). For $n=2$, van Kampen / cellular $\pi_1$ adds the relation given by the attaching word.
Frontier: Higher van Kampen theorems (Brown–Loday, Ellis–Steiner) use cat$^n$-groups / crossed modules for $\pi_n$. Colimits of spaces do not always yield colimits of $\pi_*$ beyond dimension 1 without extra structure.

Fibration $F\hookrightarrow E\xrightarrow{p}B$ yields long exact $\cdots\to\pi_n(F)\to\pi_n(E)\to\pi_n(B)\xrightarrow{\partial}\pi_{n-1}(F)\to\cdots$.

Fibration Homotopy LES

  • $F\to E\to B$ Serre/Hurewicz fibration
  • $\partial:\pi_n(B)\to\pi_{n-1}(F)$ connecting map
  • Exactness at every term of the LES
  • Hopf: $\pi_n(S^3)\cong\pi_n(S^2)$ for $n\ge 3$
  • $\Omega B\to PB\to B$ path fibration
  • $\pi_n(B)\cong\pi_{n-1}(\Omega B)$
Connecting homomorphism: Represent $\alpha\in\pi_n(B)$ by $f:(D^n,\partial D^n)\to(B,b_0)$. Lift to $\tilde f:D^n\to E$ with $\tilde f|_{\partial}=?$ landing in $F$; restrict gives $\partial\alpha\in\pi_{n-1}(F)$.
Hopf calculation: From $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$, exactness and $\pi_k(S^1)=0$ for $k\ge 2$ force $\pi_n(S^3)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_n(S^2)$ for $n\ge 3$, and $\partial:\pi_2(S^2)\to\pi_1(S^1)$ is $\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbb{Z}$.
Frontier: Quasi-fibrations (Dold–Thom), fiber sequences in $\infty$-categories, and Blakers–Massey excision control how far the LES remains exact after replacements.

Weak homotopy equivalence: $f$ induces isomorphisms on all $\pi_n$. Whitehead: for CW complexes, weak $\Rightarrow$ homotopy equivalence.

Whitehead & Weak Equivalences

  • Weak eq: $f_*:\pi_n(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_n(Y)$ $\forall n$
  • Whitehead thm: CW + weak $\Rightarrow$ $f$ homotopy eq
  • CW approx: $\Gamma X\to X$ weak eq, $\Gamma X$ CW
  • Model cat: Quillen structure on Top / sSet
  • Not iff: non-CW spaces can fail converse
  • $H_*$ isos do not imply weak eq (Hurewicz gap)
Whitehead (1949): If $X,Y$ are connected CW complexes and $f:X\to Y$ induces isomorphisms on all $\pi_n$, then $f$ is a homotopy equivalence (has a homotopy inverse).
CW approximation: Every space $X$ admits a CW complex $\Gamma X$ and a weak equivalence $\gamma:\Gamma X\to X$. Homotopy theory of spaces is essentially that of CW complexes up to weak equivalence (Quillen model structure).
Frontier: In $\infty$-categories, weak equivalences become equivalences in the localization. Cellular approximation, Postnikov towers, and obstruction theory refine when maps lift up to homotopy.

Serre spectral sequence: $E^2_{p,q}=H_p(B;H_q(F))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(E)$ for a Serre fibration $F\to E\to B$ (orientable local coeffs).

Spectral Sequences (Spaces)

  • $E^r_{p,q}$ pages with $d_r:E^r_{p,q}\to E^r_{p-r,q+r-1}$
  • $E^{r+1}=H(E^r,d_r)$
  • Serre: $E^2=H_*(B;H_*(F))\Rightarrow H_*(E)$
  • Atiyah–Hirzebruch for generalized cohomology
  • ADAMS SS for stable stems (frontier)
  • Convergence to associated graded of filtration
Serre SS intuition: Filter total space by preimages of skeleta of the base. The $E^2$ page packages base homology with fiber homology coefficients; differentials encode twisting and attaching.
Hopf example: For $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$, only a few $E^2$ slots are nonzero; $d_2$ kills the fiber class against the base fundamental class, recovering $H_*(S^3)$.
Frontier: Adams, ANSS, motivic SS, and synthetic spectra compute stable homotopy groups. This tab stays "lite" — full machine computations live in research software (e.g. Ext charts).

Mapping class group $\mathrm{Mod}(S)=\pi_0\mathrm{Homeo}^+(S)$: isotopy classes of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of surface $S$.

Mapping Class Groups

  • $\mathrm{Mod}(S_g)=\pi_0\mathrm{Diff}^+(S_g)$
  • $\mathrm{Mod}(T^2)\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$
  • Dehn twist $\tau_c$ about simple closed curve $c$
  • Lickorish–Humphries finite Dehn generators
  • Action on $H_1$ via symplectic rep $\Psi$
  • Torelli $\mathcal{I}_g=\ker\Psi$
Dehn twist: Cut along simple closed curve $c$, rotate one side by $2\pi$, reglue. Generates (with enough curves) the full mapping class group of an orientable surface.
Torus: $\mathrm{Mod}(T^2)\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ acts on $H_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ by linear automorphisms of determinant 1. Farey tessellation encodes the curve complex of $T^2$.
Frontier: Curve complex hyperbolicity (Masur–Minsky), Nielsen–Thurston classification, Mirzakhani volumes of $\mathcal{M}_g$, and TQFT representations of mapping class groups.

Persistent homology tracks births/deaths of homology classes across a filtration — stable shape descriptors for data (TDA).

Persistent Homology / TDA

  • Filtration $K_{\varepsilon}\subset K_{\varepsilon'}$ for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon'$
  • Vietoris–Rips $R_\varepsilon(X)$: cliques on $\varepsilon$-graph
  • Barcode intervals $[b,d)$ of feature lifetime
  • PD points $(b,d)$ in birth–death plane
  • Stability bottleneck distance $\le$ Gromov–Hausdorff
  • $\mathrm{PH}_k$ module over $\mathbb{k}[t]$ (structure thm)
Pipeline: Point cloud $\to$ nested complexes (Rips/Čech/alpha) $\to$ chain complexes $\to$ persistent homology barcodes. Long bars = robust features; short bars = noise.
Stability theorem: The bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams is bounded by the Gromov–Hausdorff distance of the underlying metric spaces (Cohen–Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer).
Frontier: Multiparameter persistence (no complete discrete invariant), sheaf TDA, persistent Laplacians, and ML on persistence images / landscapes.
