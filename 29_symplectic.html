Action-Angle Variables (θ₁, θ₂)
In action-angle coordinates the dynamics is linear: θ̇ᵢ = ωᵢ = const. The torus [0,2π]² is the fundamental domain. The trajectory wraps as a straight line of slope ω₂/ω₁.

Poisson Bracket

{f,g} = ∂f/∂q · ∂g/∂p − ∂f/∂p · ∂g/∂q

The Poisson bracket is the Lie bracket of Hamiltonian vector fields: X_{f,g} = [X_f, X_g]. Key identities:

{q,p} = 1 (canonical)

{H,f} = ḟ (equation of motion)

{f,g} = −{g,f} (antisymmetry)

{{f,g},h} + {{g,h},f} + {{h,f},g} = 0 (Jacobi)

Canonical Transformations

(q,p) → (Q,P) with dQ∧dP = dq∧dp

A transformation is canonical if it preserves the symplectic form ω. Equivalently its Jacobian matrix J satisfies JᵀΩJ = Ω where Ω is the standard symplectic matrix.

The left panel shows trajectories in (q,p) coordinates; the right panel shows the same trajectories in the new (Q,P) coordinates. Shapes deform but enclosed areas are preserved.

Four Generating Functions

F₁(q,Q): p=∂F₁/∂q, P=−∂F₁/∂Q

F₂(q,P): p=∂F₂/∂q, Q=∂F₂/∂P

F₃(p,Q): q=−∂F₃/∂p, P=−∂F₃/∂Q

F₄(p,P): q=−∂F₄/∂p, Q=∂F₄/∂P

Example: Q=q², P=p/2q is generated by F₂(q,P) = q²P, giving p=∂F₂/∂q = 2qP = 2q·(p/2q) = p ✓ and Q=∂F₂/∂P = q²✓.

Physical significance: Every canonical transformation corresponds to a symmetry or a change of representation. The action-angle transformation is the canonical transformation that linearizes integrable dynamics.

Action Variables

Iᵢ = (1/2π) ∮ pᵢ dqᵢ = Eᵢ/ωᵢ

For the 2D harmonic oscillator H = ω₁I₁ + ω₂I₂. The action Iᵢ = Eᵢ/ωᵢ is the area enclosed by the i-th orbit in the (qᵢ, pᵢ) sub-plane divided by 2π — a topological invariant.

Angle Variables

θ̇ᵢ = ∂H/∂Iᵢ = ωᵢ = const

In action-angle coordinates (I,θ) the Hamiltonian depends only on the actions: H = H(I). Dynamics is linear on the torus: θᵢ(t) = ωᵢt + θᵢ(0).

Rational vs Irrational Frequencies

ω₁/ω₂ ∈ ℚ: closed orbit (Lissajous figure) — the trajectory returns to its starting point after a finite time.

ω₁/ω₂ ∉ ℚ: dense orbit — fills the torus ergodically. The golden ratio φ=(1+√5)/2 is the "most irrational" — KAM tori with this frequency are the last to break.

KAM Theorem

H = H₀(I) + εH₁(q,I,t)

For small perturbations ε, most tori survive if the frequencies satisfy the Diophantine condition |k·ω| ≥ γ|k|⁻ᵛ. Resonant tori break first; the golden-ratio torus is the last survivor. KAM theory proves persistence of quasi-periodic motion in nearly-integrable systems.

Geometric Quantization

Quantization is not merely "put hats on classical quantities." Geometric quantization shows how quantum mechanics emerges from symplectic geometry in three stages:

1. Prequantization

L → phase space, curv(∇) = ω/ħ

Construct a complex line bundle L over phase space whose curvature 2-form equals ω/ħ. Sections ψ of L are prequantum wave functions. The Dirac quantization condition: ω/2πħ must have integer cohomology class — this is why ∮ p dq = nħ.

2. Polarization

Lagrangian submanifold: T*Q ⊂ T*M

A polarization selects half the phase space directions (a Lagrangian foliation). Kähler polarization gives holomorphic wave functions; real polarization (vertical = momentum directions) recovers position-space QM: ψ(q).

3. Bohr-Sommerfeld Condition

∮_γ p dq = nħ, n ∈ ℤ

Only orbits γ on the torus for which the action integral is an integer multiple of ħ contribute. This selects quantized tori. For the harmonic oscillator: I = E/ω = nħ → E_n = nħω (or (n+½)ħω with metaplectic correction).

The winding number of the prequantum connection around the torus is the quantum number n — quantum mechanics is topology.

Moment Maps

μ: M → 𝔤* , d⟨μ,ξ⟩ = ı_{X_ξ} ω

A Hamiltonian G-action on (M,ω) admits a moment map μ: M → 𝔤* whose components generate the infinitesimal action: X_ξ = X_{⟨μ,ξ⟩}. Equivariance: μ(g·p) = Ad*_g μ(p) (or affine with cocycle).

Marsden–Weinstein Reduction

M_red = μ⁻¹(0)/G   (regular value)

If 0 is regular and G acts freely on μ⁻¹(0), then M_red inherits a unique symplectic form ω_red with π*ω_red = i*ω. Dimension: dim M − 2 dim G.

Classic examples

S¹ ↷ S²: rotation about z-axis; μ = height. Levels are latitude circles; reduced spaces are points — recovers coadjoint orbits of SU(2).

T*G // G: cotangent-lifted left action reduces to 𝔤* with Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau form.

Frontier: singular reduction (Sjamaar–Lerman), Hamiltonian groupoids, infinite-dim reduction for fluids/Yang–Mills, derived symplectic reduction (PTVV).

Tautological 1-Form

θ_α(v) = α(dπ(v)) ,   α ∈ T*_q Q

On T*Q the Liouville / tautological 1-form θ satisfies: at α ∈ T*_qQ, θ_α(v) = α(π_* v). In coordinates θ = Σ p_i dq^i. Then ω = −dθ is the canonical symplectic structure — natural, exact, and coordinate-independent.

Liouville Vector Field

E = Σ p_i ∂_{p_i} ,   L_E ω = ω ,   ı_E ω = θ

Fiberwise radial dilation is conformal for ω. Exact symplectic manifolds are those admitting a global Liouville field (convex contact-type boundary).

Cotangent Lift

Any diffeomorphism φ: Q→Q lifts to a symplectomorphism T*φ: T*Q→T*Q with (T*φ)*θ = θ. Generating function of type F₂: S(q,P) = P·φ(q).

Frontier: Weinstein conjecture on Reeb orbits of contact-type boundaries of T*Q; generating functions quadratic at infinity (Viterbo); microlocal sheaves and Fukaya categories of cotangents.

Compatible Almost Complex Structures

J² = −Id ,   ω(Ju,Jv)=ω(u,v) ,   g(u,v)=ω(u,Jv)>0

The space 𝒥(M,ω) of ω-compatible almost complex structures is contractible and nonempty (Gromov). Every (M,ω) is thus almost Kähler: (ω,J,g) form a compatible triple.

Integrability → Kähler

N_J(X,Y)=[JX,JY]−J[JX,Y]−J[X,JY]−[X,Y]

Newlander–Nirenberg: N_J=0 ⇔ J integrable (complex structure). If additionally dω=0 (always symplectic), the manifold is Kähler.

Why it matters for symplectic topology

Pseudo-holomorphic curves (J-holomorphic maps u: (Σ,j)→(M,J)) are the backbone of Gromov–Witten, Floer, and Fukaya theory. Their energy is topological: E(u)=∫u*ω.

Frontier: almost complex vs tamed J; inflation and packing; Donaldson’s approximately holomorphic sections; SYZ mirror symmetry.

Contact Structures

ξ = ker α ,   α∧(dα)^n ≠ 0

A contact structure is a maximally non-integrable hyperplane field. In ℝ³, α = dz − y dx is the standard tight structure. Contact planes twist along the z-direction; Frobenius fails exactly by dα|ξ being symplectic on ξ.

Reeb Vector Field

ı_R dα = 0 ,   α(R)=1

Weinstein conjecture: every closed contact manifold has a closed Reeb orbit (proved in 3D by Taubes via Seiberg–Witten). Symplectization ℝ×Y carries ω = d(e^t α).

Legendrian Submanifolds

Λ ⊂ (Y,ξ) is Legendrian if TΛ ⊂ ξ. In ℝ³, front projection to xz-plane has cusps; tb (Thurston–Bennequin) and rot are classical invariants. Legendrian contact homology (Chekanov–Eliashberg) is a Floer-type invariant.

Frontier: tight vs overtwisted (Eliashberg), Giroux torsion, contact homology / SFT, embedded contact homology = Seiberg–Witten, open books.

Poisson Manifolds

{f,g} = π(df,dg) ,   [π,π]_SN = 0

A bivector π with vanishing Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket induces a Lie algebra on C^∞(M). Symplectic leaves (integrating the characteristic distribution Im π^#) carry the induced symplectic form. Symplectic geometry is the nondegenerate special case.

Lie–Poisson Structure

{f,g}_±(μ) = ±⟨μ,[df,dg]⟩ on 𝔤*

Coadjoint orbits are the symplectic leaves (KKS form). Rigid body Euler equations are Lie–Poisson on so(3)*.

Dirac Structures

L ⊂ TM⊕T*M ,   maximal isotropic + involutive for Courant

Courant algebroid (TM⊕T*M, ⟨,⟩, ⟦,⟧). Dirac structures unify Poisson, pre-symplectic, and constraints (Dirac’s theory of constraints). Morphisms: Courant morphisms / Lagrangian correspondences.

Frontier: generalized complex geometry (Hitchin–Gualtieri), Poisson–Lie groups, deformation quantization (Kontsevich), shifted symplectic / PTVV derived stacks.

Gromov Non-Squeezing

∃ symplectic emb. B^{2n}(R) ↪ Z^{2n}(r) ⇔ R ≤ r

The cylinder Z(r) = B²(r)×ℝ^{2n−2} has infinite volume, yet cannot contain a ball of radius > r via a symplectomorphism. Symplectic maps are more rigid than volume-preserving maps. Proof uses J-holomorphic curves: a curve through the image forces area ≥ πR².

Symplectic Capacities

c_G(U) = sup{πR² : B(R) ↪ U}   (Gromov width)

Axioms: monotonicity, conformality c(φ(U))=|α|c(U) for φ*ω=αω, and normalization c(B(1))=c(Z(1))=π. Ekeland–Hofer, Hofer–Zehnder, and spectral capacities from Floer theory refine the story.

Packing

How many equal balls fit symplectically into a ball / CP^n? Gromov–McDuff–Polterovich packing numbers encode quantum cohomology constraints.

Frontier: Viterbo conjecture on capacities of convex bodies, symplectic camel, quantitative embedding (Hind–Kerman), barcodes as capacity spectrum.
