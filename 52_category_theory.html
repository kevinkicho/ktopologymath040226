What is a Category?

A category $\mathbf{C}$ consists of:

  • A collection of objects $\mathrm{ob}(\mathbf{C})$
  • For each pair $A,B$: a set of morphisms $\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)$
  • An identity $\mathrm{id}_A: A\to A$ for each object
  • A composition law: if $f:A\to B$ and $g:B\to C$ then $g\circ f:A\to C$

Axioms

$$\mathrm{id}_B\circ f = f = f\circ\mathrm{id}_A$$
$$(h\circ g)\circ f = h\circ(g\circ f)$$

Examples

Set
sets & functions
Grp
groups & homomorphisms
Top
spaces & continuous maps
Vect
vector spaces & linear maps
1
the trivial category with one object

Commutative Diagrams

A diagram commutes when all paths between two objects compose to the same morphism. This is the key language of modern mathematics.

$$g\circ f = h\circ k \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{square commutes}$$

Penrose Connection

Category theory unifies disparate mathematical structures under one roof. Penrose emphasizes that the "sameness" of mathematical structures — isomorphism — is categorical at heart.

Functors

A functor $F:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}$ maps objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, preserving structure:

$$F(\mathrm{id}_A) = \mathrm{id}_{F(A)}$$
$$F(g\circ f) = F(g)\circ F(f)$$

Functors are the "structure-preserving maps" between categories.

Natural Transformations

A natural transformation $\eta: F\Rightarrow G$ assigns to each object $A\in\mathbf{C}$ a morphism $\eta_A: F(A)\to G(A)$ such that for every $f:A\to B$:

$$\eta_B\circ F(f) = G(f)\circ\eta_A$$

This is the naturality square. Natural transformations are morphisms between functors.

Examples

$\pi_1$: fundamental group functor $\mathbf{Top}_*\to\mathbf{Grp}$

$H_n$: homology functor $\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Ab}$

Forgetful: $U:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ "forgets" the group structure

det: $\det: GL_n\to\mathbb{R}^*$ is a natural transformation between functors $\mathbf{Ring}\to\mathbf{Grp}$

Yoneda Lemma

The deepest theorem in category theory. For any functor $F:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ and object $A$:

$$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(A,-),\, F)\;\cong\; F(A)$$

Natural transformations from the representable functor $\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ to $F$ are in natural bijection with elements of $F(A)$.

Key insight: "An object is completely determined by how other objects map into it." The Yoneda embedding $A\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ is fully faithful — it preserves and reflects all structure.

$$\mathbf{C}\hookrightarrow[\mathbf{C}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$$

Yoneda in Physics

The Yoneda perspective underlies holography: a bulk geometry is determined by boundary correlators — exactly the statement that the object (bulk) is determined by morphisms into it (boundary maps). Also central to algebraic geometry: a scheme is determined by its functor of points.

The Big Picture

Categories, functors, and natural transformations form a 2-category. Saunders Mac Lane: "Categories were defined in order to define functors, functors in order to define natural transformations."

Universal Properties

A universal property characterizes an object by the morphisms into or out of it — up to unique isomorphism.

Product $A\times B$

$$\forall Z,\, \exists!\langle f,g\rangle: Z\to A\times B$$
$$\pi_1\circ\langle f,g\rangle = f,\quad \pi_2\circ\langle f,g\rangle = g$$

In $\mathbf{Set}$: Cartesian product. In $\mathbf{Top}$: product topology. In $\mathbf{Grp}$: direct product.

Coproduct $A+B$

The dual — arrows reversed. In $\mathbf{Set}$: disjoint union. In $\mathbf{Grp}$: free product. In $\mathbf{Top}$: disjoint union topology.

Pullback (Fiber Product)

$$A\times_C B = \{(a,b)\mid f(a)=g(b)\}$$

The "overlap" of $A$ and $B$ over $C$. Arises in algebraic geometry as fiber products of schemes.

Adjoint Functors $F\dashv G$

$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(FA,B)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(A,GB)$$

A natural bijection between hom-sets. Equivalently: there exist natural transformations called the unit and counit:

$$\eta: 1_{\mathbf{C}}\Rightarrow GF \quad\text{(unit)}$$
$$\varepsilon: FG\Rightarrow 1_{\mathbf{D}} \quad\text{(counit)}$$

satisfying the triangle identities: $(G\varepsilon)\circ(\eta G)=\mathrm{id}_G$ and $(\varepsilon F)\circ(F\eta)=\mathrm{id}_F$.

Examples

  • Free $\dashv$ Forgetful: $\mathrm{Free}:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Grp}$, $U:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$. Unit $\eta_S: S\to U(\mathrm{Free}(S))$ includes $S$ as generators.
  • Tensor $\otimes$ $\dashv$ internal hom $[{-},{-}]$: underlies currying in type theory
  • $\Sigma\dashv\pi$ in dependent types: existential $\dashv$ universal quantifier
  • Suspension $\dashv$ loop space in homotopy theory
  • Quantization $\dashv$ classical limit in physics

"Adjoint functors arise everywhere." — Saunders Mac Lane

What is a Topos?

A topos is a category that behaves like $\mathbf{Set}$: it has finite limits, exponentials, and a subobject classifier $\Omega$.

Subobject Classifier

$$\chi_S: X\to\Omega,\quad \chi_S(x)=\begin{cases}\top & x\in S\\ \bot & x\notin S\end{cases}$$

In $\mathbf{Set}$, $\Omega=\{\top,\bot\}$. A subobject $S\hookrightarrow X$ is classified by its characteristic function into $\Omega$.

Intuitionistic Logic

In a general topos, $\Omega$ is a Heyting algebra — it may have more than 2 truth values. Key: $\neg\neg p\neq p$ in general ("not not true" ≠ "true").

$$\Omega_{\mathrm{Sh}(X)} = \text{open sets of }X$$

Sheaves

For a topological space $X$, a sheaf $F$ assigns a set $F(U)$ (local sections) to each open $U\subseteq X$, with compatible restriction maps:

$$F:\mathrm{Open}(X)^{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$$

Sheaves on $X$ form a topos $\mathrm{Sh}(X)$.

Physics: Gauge Fields as Sheaves

A gauge field (connection on a principal bundle) is precisely a sheaf of local trivializations. The transition functions encode the non-triviality — and are exactly the cocycles in Čech cohomology.

$$A_\mu\;dx^\mu \;\longleftrightarrow\; \text{connection 1-form on }P\to M$$

Presheaf Toposes & Many Truth Values

For the category $\mathbf{C}$, the presheaf topos $[\mathbf{C}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$ has a rich subobject classifier. A sieve on object $U$ is a collection of morphisms into $U$ closed under precomposition. Then:

$$\Omega(U)=\{\text{sieves on }U\}$$

This has many more than 2 truth values — modal and temporal logics naturally arise.

Forcing & Cohen Independence

Paul Cohen's 1963 proof that the Continuum Hypothesis ($\neg$CH) is independent of ZFC uses a forcing topos. The forcing poset $\mathbb{P}$ defines a Grothendieck topos $\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbb{P})$ in which:

  • Truth values = upward-closed sets in $\mathbb{P}$ (a Heyting algebra)
  • Excluded middle fails: $p\vee\neg p\neq\top$ for some conditions $p$
  • CH can be made false by adding generic reals

Topos theory gives a clean conceptual framework for set-theoretic forcing.

ZX-Calculus & Quantum Theory

Quantum mechanics can be formulated as a symmetric monoidal category with dagger structure $(\dagger)$. The ZX-calculus provides a graphical language for quantum circuits where:

  • Z-spiders (green) = Z-basis operations
  • X-spiders (red) = X-basis operations
  • Wires = qubit Hilbert spaces

Penrose: "The categorical viewpoint reveals the deep structural unity of physics and mathematics."

n-Categories & TQFTs — cobordisms as morphisms, Atiyah-Segal axioms, Baez-Dolan cobordism hypothesis
1x
Mode: 2-Categories  |  Objects, 1-morphisms, and 2-morphisms with vertical and horizontal composition

2-Categories

A 2-category has three levels:

  • 0-cells (objects): $A, B, C, \ldots$
  • 1-cells (morphisms): $f: A\to B$
  • 2-cells (morphisms between morphisms): $\alpha: f\Rightarrow g$

2-cells compose in two ways: vertically ($\alpha$ then $\beta$) and horizontally (using the interchange law).

$$(\beta\cdot\alpha)\circ(\delta\cdot\gamma)=(\beta\circ\delta)\cdot(\alpha\circ\gamma)$$

Atiyah-Segal TQFT Axioms

A topological quantum field theory in dimension $n$ is a symmetric monoidal functor:

$$Z:\mathbf{Cob}_n\to\mathbf{Vect}_\mathbb{C}$$

where $\mathbf{Cob}_n$ has closed $(n{-}1)$-manifolds as objects and $n$-dimensional cobordisms as morphisms.

  • A circle $S^1$ maps to a vector space $Z(S^1)=H$ (Hilbert space)
  • A pair-of-pants cobordism maps to a linear map $H\otimes H\to H$ (multiplication)
  • A cap (disk bounding $S^1$) maps to a trace $H\to\mathbb{C}$

Cobordism Hypothesis (Baez-Dolan)

Fully extended TQFTs $Z:\mathbf{Cob}_n^{fr}\to\mathcal{C}$ (with framings) are classified by fully dualizable objects in $\mathcal{C}$:

$$\{\text{fully ext. fr. TQFTs}\}\;\cong\;\{\text{fully dualizable obj. in }\mathcal{C}\}$$

Proved by Lurie (2009) using $(\infty,n)$-categories. This encodes the entire TQFT from the value on a point.

A-model & B-model

Witten's topological string theories are TQFTs on Riemann surfaces:

  • A-model: functor from $\mathbf{Cob}_2\to\mathbf{Fuk}(X)$ (Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold $X$). Counts holomorphic curves — gives Gromov-Witten invariants.
  • B-model: functor to $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X^\vee)$ (derived category of coherent sheaves on the mirror $X^\vee$). Mirror symmetry is the equivalence $\mathbf{Fuk}(X)\simeq D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X^\vee)$ — Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry conjecture.

$(\infty,n)$-Categories

An $(\infty,1)$-category has objects, morphisms, homotopies between morphisms, homotopies between homotopies, … with all higher morphisms invertible. These are modeled by quasi-categories (Joyal) or complete Segal spaces (Rezk). The $(\infty,1)$-category of spaces is the homotopy hypothesis: groupoids $\leftrightarrow$ homotopy types.

Lurie's "Higher Topos Theory" and "Higher Algebra" provide the technical foundation for modern derived algebraic geometry and factorization algebras in QFT.

Yoneda Lemma: Nat(Hom(A,–), F) ≅ F(A) — the natural bijection in action
1x
Nat(Hom(A,–), F) ≅ F(A)  |  η ↔ x ∈ F(A)

Yoneda Lemma

$$\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(A,-),\, F) \cong F(A)$$

For any functor $F:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{Set}$ and object $A\in\mathbf{C}$, natural transformations from the representable functor $\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ to $F$ are in natural bijection with elements of $F(A)$.

Yoneda Embedding

$$\mathbf{C} \hookrightarrow [\mathbf{C}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$$

The functor $A \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ is fully faithful: it preserves and reflects all categorical structure. An object is completely determined by how other objects map into it.

Key Insight

The Yoneda Lemma is the "representation theorem" of category theory. It says that every functor $F$ is determined by its values on representable functors. This is the categorical analog of the Riesz representation theorem in functional analysis.

Proof Sketch

Given a natural transformation $\eta: \mathrm{Hom}(A,-)\Rightarrow F$, evaluate at $A$: $\eta_A(\mathrm{id}_A) \in F(A)$. Conversely, given $x\in F(A)$, define $\eta_B(f) = F(f)(x)$ for each $f:A\to B$. These two maps are inverses.

Kan Extensions: left (Lan) and right (Ran) along a functor K: C → D
1x
(Lan_K F)(d) = colim_{c → K(c)=d} F(c)  |  Left Kan Extension

Kan Extensions

Given functors $K:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}$ and $F:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{E}$, a left Kan extension $\mathrm{Lan}_K F:\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{E}$ is the "best approximation" to extending $F$ along $K$ from the left.

$$(\mathrm{Lan}_K F)(d) = \mathrm{colim}_{c\in K\downarrow d} F(c)$$

The colimit is taken over the comma category $K\downarrow d$: pairs $(c, f: K(c)\to d)$.

Right Kan Extension

$$(\mathrm{Ran}_K F)(d) = \lim_{c\in d\downarrow K} F(c)$$

The limit is over the comma category $d\downarrow K$: pairs $(c, f: d\to K(c))$.

Universal Property

There is a natural transformation $\eta: F \Rightarrow \mathrm{Lan}_K F \circ K$ that is initial among all such: any other $G:\mathbf{D}\to\mathbf{E}$ with $\alpha: F\Rightarrow G\circ K$ factors uniquely through $\eta$.

Examples

  • Yoneda: $\mathrm{Lan}_A \mathrm{Hom}(A,-) \cong \mathrm{id}$
  • Day convolution: monoidal structure on presheaves
  • Ind-completion: $\mathrm{Lan}$ of finite diagrams
Monad (T, η, μ) — Eilenberg-Moore algebras — Kleisli category — adjunction factorization
1x
Monad: $T = G\circ F$  |  $\mu: T^2 \to T$  |  $\eta: \mathrm{Id} \to T$  |  Maybe

Monads

A monad on a category $\mathbf{C}$ is a triple $(T, \eta, \mu)$ where $T:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}$ is an endofunctor, and $\eta: \mathrm{Id}\Rightarrow T$, $\mu: T^2\Rightarrow T$ are natural transformations satisfying:

$$\mu\circ T\mu = \mu\circ\mu T \quad\text{(associativity)}$$
$$\mu\circ\eta T = \mathrm{id} = \mu\circ T\eta \quad\text{(unit laws)}$$

Eilenberg-Moore Algebras

An algebra for a monad $T$ is a pair $(a, \alpha: T(a)\to a)$ satisfying $\alpha\circ\eta_a = \mathrm{id}_a$ and $\alpha\circ\mu_a = \alpha\circ T\alpha$. The category of algebras $\mathbf{C}^T$ is the Eilenberg-Moore category.

Kleisli Category

The Kleisli category $\mathbf{C}_T$ has the same objects as $\mathbf{C}$, but morphisms $A\to B$ in $\mathbf{C}_T$ are morphisms $A\to T(B)$ in $\mathbf{C}$. Composition uses the monad structure: $g\circ_T f = \mu\circ T(g)\circ f$.

Adjunction Factorization

$$F^T \dashv G^T : \mathbf{C}^T \to \mathbf{C}$$

Every monad arises from an adjunction $F\dashv G$ with $T = G\circ F$. The Eilenberg-Moore category gives the terminal such factorization; the Kleisli category gives the initial one.

Examples

  • Maybe: $T(X) = X\sqcup\{\star\}$, adds failure
  • List: $T(X) = \bigsqcup_n X^n$, non-determinism
  • State: $T(X) = (S\times X)^S$, stateful computation
  • Reader: $T(X) = X^E$, environment reading

Limits & Colimits

A diagram of shape $J$ is a functor $F:J\to\mathbf{C}$. A cone from $L$ to $F$ is a natural transformation $\Delta L\Rightarrow F$. The limit is a terminal cone.

$$\mathrm{Hom}(X,\lim F)\;\cong\;\mathrm{Cone}(X,F)$$

Colimits

Dual: a cocone $F\Rightarrow\Delta C$ is initial when $C=\mathrm{colim}\,F$.

$$\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{colim}\,F,Y)\;\cong\;\mathrm{Cocone}(F,Y)$$

Special shapes

  • Products / coproducts: discrete $J$
  • Equalizers: parallel pair $\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet$
  • Pullbacks: cospan $\bullet\to\bullet\leftarrow\bullet$
  • Filtered colimits: commute with finite limits in $\mathbf{Set}$

Completeness

$\mathbf{C}$ is complete if all small limits exist; cocomplete if all small colimits exist. $\mathbf{Set}$, $\mathbf{Top}$, $\mathbf{Ab}$, and every topos are complete and cocomplete.

Frontier

Weighted (co)limits: $\lim^W F=\int_j [Wj,Fj]$. In $\infty$-categories, limits are homotopy limits (homotopy coherent cones). Accessible categories: generated under filtered colimits by a small subcategory.

Adjoint Functors

An adjunction $F\dashv G$ is a natural family of bijections

$$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(FA,B)\;\cong\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(A,GB)$$

Unit & Counit

$$\eta:1_{\mathbf{C}}\Rightarrow GF,\qquad\varepsilon:FG\Rightarrow 1_{\mathbf{D}}$$

Triangle identities: $(G\varepsilon)\circ(\eta G)=\mathrm{id}_G$ and $(\varepsilon F)\circ(F\eta)=\mathrm{id}_F$.

Preservation theorems

  • RAPL: right adjoints preserve limits
  • LAPC: left adjoints preserve colimits

Classic examples

  • Free group $\dashv$ forgetful $\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$
  • $-\otimes A\dashv\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$ in modules
  • $\Sigma\dashv\Omega$ (suspension–loop) in spaces
  • Sheafification $\dashv$ inclusion of sheaves

Frontier

Adjoint functor theorem (Freyd): under solution-set / accessibility hypotheses, continuous functors from complete locally presentable categories have left adjoints. In higher categories: adjoints are unique up to contractible choice; Lurie's adjoint functor theorem for presentable $\infty$-categories.

Ends & Coends

For a bifunctor $T:\mathbf{C}^{op}\times\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}$, the end is a universal dinatural (extranatural) wedge into $T(c,c)$:

$$\int_c T(c,c)\;=\;\mathrm{eq}\Big(\prod_c T(c,c)\rightrightarrows\prod_{f:c\to c'}T(c,c')\Big)$$

Coends

$$\int^c T(c,c)\;=\;\mathrm{coeq}\Big(\coprod_{f}T(c',c)\rightrightarrows\coprod_c T(c,c)\Big)$$

Yoneda as (co)end

$$F(c)\;\cong\;\int^d\mathrm{Hom}(d,c)\times F(d)$$

The coend form of the density / Yoneda lemma.

Day convolution

If $(\mathbf{C},\otimes)$ is monoidal, Day convolution on $[\mathbf{C}^{op},\mathbf{Set}]$ is the coend

$$(F\star G)(c)=\int^{a,b}F(a)\times G(b)\times\mathrm{Hom}(a\otimes b,c)$$

Frontier

Ends compute natural transformations: $\mathrm{Nat}(F,G)=\int_c\mathrm{Hom}(Fc,Gc)$. Coends appear in geometric realization, tensor products of functors, and the calculus of mates. Higher ends: weighted (co)limits in enriched and $\infty$-settings.

Monoidal Categories

A monoidal category $(\mathbf{C},\otimes,I,\alpha,\lambda,\rho)$ has a tensor bifunctor, unit object, and coherent natural isomorphisms

$$\alpha_{A,B,C}:(A\otimes B)\otimes C\xrightarrow{\sim}A\otimes(B\otimes C)$$

Coherence (Mac Lane)

Pentagon for $\alpha$ and triangle for unitors: all diagrams built from $\alpha,\lambda,\rho$ commute. Strict monoidal categories are equivalent to non-strict ones (strictification).

Braided & symmetric

$$\sigma_{A,B}:A\otimes B\to B\otimes A$$

Hexagon axioms. Symmetric: $\sigma_{B,A}\circ\sigma_{A,B}=\mathrm{id}$.

Duals & rigidity

Left dual $A^*$ with evaluation/coevaluation (cups/caps). Compact closed / rigid monoidal categories underpin TQFTs and quantum groups.

Frontier

String diagrams (Joyal–Street, Penrose): morphisms as planar graphs; coherence = planar isotopy. Fusion categories, modular tensor categories (Reshetikhin–Turaev), and higher monoidal structures ($\mathrm{E}_n$-algebras) in $\infty$-categories.

Enriched Categories

Fix a monoidal category $\mathcal{V}$. A $\mathcal{V}$-category has objects and, for each pair, an object $\mathbf{C}(A,B)\in\mathcal{V}$, with composition

$$\mathbf{C}(B,C)\otimes\mathbf{C}(A,B)\to\mathbf{C}(A,C)$$

and units $I\to\mathbf{C}(A,A)$ in $\mathcal{V}$, associative up to $\mathcal{V}$'s structure.

Examples

  • $\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Set}$: ordinary categories
  • $\mathcal{V}=[0,\infty]$ (Lawvere): generalized metric spaces, $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$
  • $\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Ab}$: preadditive categories
  • $\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{Ch}(\mathbf{Ab})$: dg-categories
  • $\mathcal{V}=\mathbf{sSet}$ / $\mathbf{Top}$: simplicially / topologically enriched

Change of base

A lax monoidal $F:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}$ induces $F_*$ on $\mathcal{V}$-categories (e.g. $\pi_0$ from simplicial enrichment).

Frontier

Weighted limits, $\mathcal{V}$-ends, and Kelly's enriched category theory. Spectral enrichment, $\infty$-operads, and enrichment in presentable monoidal $\infty$-categories (Lurie HA).

Model Categories (Quillen)

A model category has three classes of morphisms — weak equivalences $W$, cofibrations $\mathrm{Cof}$, fibrations $\mathrm{Fib}$ — closed under retracts, with 2-out-of-3 for $W$, lifting axioms, and two factorizations.

$$\mathrm{Cof}\cap W\;\perp\;\mathrm{Fib},\qquad\mathrm{Cof}\;\perp\;\mathrm{Fib}\cap W$$

Homotopy category

$\mathrm{Ho}(\mathbf{C})=W^{-1}\mathbf{C}$ localizes at weak equivalences. On cofibrant–fibrant objects, morphisms in Ho are homotopy classes of maps.

Examples

  • $\mathbf{Top}$ / $\mathbf{sSet}$: Quillen equivalence (singular / realization)
  • $\mathbf{Ch}_{\ge0}(R)$: projective / injective model structures
  • Simplicial sets: Kan–Quillen model structure

Quillen adjunctions

$F\dashv G$ is Quillen if $F$ preserves cofibrations and acyclic cofibrations (equivalently $G$ preserves fibrations and acyclic fibrations). Derived functors $\mathbb{L}F,\mathbb{R}G$ on Ho.

Frontier

Combinatorial / accessible model categories, Bousfield localization, and the comparison to $\infty$-categories: every combinatorial model category presents a presentable $\infty$-category. Hovey, Hirschhorn, Lurie HTT App. A.

∞-Categories (Lite)

An $(\infty,1)$-category has morphisms invertible up to higher cells. A standard model: quasi-categories (Boardman–Vogt, Joyal) — simplicial sets $X$ where every inner horn $\Lambda^n_i\hookrightarrow\Delta^n$ ($0<i<n$) has a filler.

$$\mathrm{Map}_X(x,y)\;=\;X_{/\bullet}\text{ with fixed ends}$$

Composition

Horn fillers give composition of 1-simplices up to a 2-simplex homotopy — no strict associativity required; higher cells supply coherent associators.

Nerve of a category

$$N(\mathbf{C})_n=\mathrm{Fun}([n],\mathbf{C})$$

$N(\mathbf{C})$ is a quasi-category; ordinary categories embed fully faithfully into $\infty$-categories.

What this tab is not

Deep $\infty$-topoi, stable $\infty$-categories, and Lurie's Higher Algebra live primarily in Ch. 18 (Infinity). Here: pure categorical lite — horns, nerves, mapping spaces, homotopy coherent diagrams.

Frontier pointers

Complete Segal spaces (Rezk), marked simplicial sets, and the equivalence of models. Limits/colimits in qcats are modeled by right/left Kan extensions along nerves of shapes.
