Quantum Hall Effect

In a 2D electron gas at low temperature and strong magnetic field, the Hall conductance is precisely quantized:

$$\sigma_{xy} = n\frac{e^2}{h}$$

The integer $n$ is a topological invariant — the Chern number — immune to disorder.

Landau Levels

$$E_n = \hbar\omega_c\!\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right),\quad \omega_c = \frac{eB}{m}$$

Electrons in a magnetic field form discrete energy levels. When the Fermi energy lies between levels, $\sigma_{xy}$ is on a plateau.

Filling Factor

$$\nu = \frac{nh}{eB}$$

Chern Number (TKNN)

$$C = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}} \Omega_n\,d^2k$$

The Berry curvature $\Omega_n$ integrated over the Brillouin zone gives an integer. This is the topological origin of quantization.

Fractional QHE

At filling $\nu = 1/3, 2/5, \ldots$ strong electron–electron interactions produce a new topological state with fractionally charged quasi-particles (anyons).

Berry Phase & Holonomy

$$\gamma_n = i\oint_C \langle u_k|\nabla_{\!k}|u_k\rangle\cdot dk$$

A geometric phase accumulated when a Bloch state $|u_k\rangle$ is adiabatically transported around a closed loop $C$ in the Brillouin zone. It is gauge-invariant for closed loops.

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}_k = i\langle u_k|\nabla_k u_k\rangle\quad\text{(Berry connection)}$$
$$\boldsymbol{\Omega} = \nabla_k\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\quad\text{(Berry curvature)}$$

For a spin-1/2 in a magnetic field, a Dirac monopole at the origin of parameter space sources the curvature. The Berry phase equals half the solid angle subtended on the Bloch sphere:

$$\gamma = \frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{solid}}}{2}$$

A non-contractible loop enclosing the monopole gives $\gamma = \pi$ — a sign flip of the wavefunction, the topological signature of Chern number $= 1$.

Holonomy

The spin vector on the Bloch sphere is parallel-transported along the loop. After completing the circuit it returns rotated by $\gamma$ — holonomy in the fiber bundle sense (Penrose §15.4).

TKNN Invariant

Thouless, Kohmoto, Nightingale & den Nijs (1982) showed the Hall conductance equals a topological integer:

$$C = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}} \Omega_{xy}(\mathbf{k})\,d^2k \in \mathbb{Z}$$

Berry Curvature

$$\Omega_{xy} = -2\,\mathrm{Im}\!\left\langle\frac{\partial u}{\partial k_x}\Bigg|\frac{\partial u}{\partial k_y}\right\rangle$$

Acts as a "magnetic field" in k-space. Sources/sinks are Weyl-point monopoles. Color map: red = positive, blue = negative curvature.

Haldane Model

A honeycomb lattice with complex next-nearest-neighbor hopping $t_2 e^{i\phi}$ can have $C = \pm 1$ without net flux — the first realization of a Chern insulator.

$$H(\mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{d}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}$$

Phase transition at $|M| = 3\sqrt{3}\,t_2|\sin\phi|$: gap closes at a Dirac point where $\Omega$ diverges.

Bulk–Boundary

$C$ counts the net number of chiral edge modes. Each carries conductance $e^2/h$, giving the quantized plateau.

Z₂ Topological Insulators

Time-reversal symmetry $\mathcal{T}^2 = -1$ (spin-1/2) forces Kramers degeneracy at time-reversal invariant momenta (TRIM): $\Gamma, M, X$.

$$\nu = \prod_{i\in\mathrm{TRIM}} \frac{\sqrt{\det[\mathbf{w}(\Lambda_i)]}}{\mathrm{Pf}[\mathbf{w}(\Lambda_i)]} = 0,1$$

Kane-Mele Model

Graphene with spin-orbit coupling: intrinsic SOC opens a gap without breaking TR. The $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant $\nu = 1$ when SOC exceeds Rashba coupling.

$$H = t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}c^\dagger_i c_j + i\lambda_{\rm SO}\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}\nu_{ij}c^\dagger_i s_z c_j$$

Topological Protection

Edge modes are helical: spin-up moves right, spin-down moves left. Backscattering requires a spin flip — forbidden by TR symmetry. Disorder cannot open a gap in the edge spectrum.

Kramers Pairs

At TRIM points, edge states come in degenerate pairs. They cannot hybridize without breaking TR, guaranteeing gapless crossings.

Giant Magnetoresistance

In alternating ferromagnet / normal-metal / ferromagnet stacks, resistance depends strongly on the relative magnetic alignment.

$$\frac{\Delta R}{R} = \frac{2P^2}{1-P^2}$$

where $P$ = spin polarization of the ferromagnet. Discovered by Fert (Fe/Cr, 1988) and independently by Grünberg.

Two-Current Model

Spin-up and spin-down electrons are independent channels. In the parallel configuration, one channel passes easily (low $\rho$) and one is scattered (high $\rho$) — overall low resistance. In antiparallel, both channels are scattered — high resistance.

Applications

  • Hard drive read heads (enabled terabyte drives)
  • Magnetic field sensors
  • MRAM (magnetic RAM)
  • Biosensors

Hard Drive Bit Reading

The read head scans magnetic domains. Domain walls cause transitions in resistance $R_P \leftrightarrow R_{AP}$, encoding bits 0 and 1.

Kitaev chain: $H = \sum[-t(c^\dagger_i c_{i+1}+h.c.) - \mu c^\dagger_i c_i + \Delta c_i c_{i+1}+h.c.]$  |  Phase: Topological

Majorana Fermions in the Kitaev Chain

$$H = \sum_i\bigl[-t(c^\dagger_i c_{i+1}+h.c.) - \mu c^\dagger_i c_i + \Delta c_i c_{i+1}+h.c.\bigr]$$

A 1D p-wave superconductor. For $|\mu| < 2t$, the system is in a topological phase with Majorana zero modes at the ends.

Majorana Operators

$$\gamma_{2i-1} = c_i + c^\dagger_i,\quad \gamma_{2i} = -i(c_i - c^\dagger_i)$$
$$\{\gamma_i,\gamma_j\} = 2\delta_{ij}$$

Each complex fermion $c_i$ splits into two real Majorana operators $\gamma_{2i-1},\gamma_{2i}$. In the topological phase, the two end Majoranas $\gamma_L,\gamma_R$ remain unpaired, forming a non-local qubit.

Topological Phase Transition

$$|\mu| = 2t \quad\text{(gap closing)}$$

At $|\mu| = 2t$ the bulk gap closes and the system transitions between trivial ($|\mu| > 2t$) and topological ($|\mu| < 2t$) phases. The end Majoranas are exponentially localized with decay length $\xi \propto 1/|\ln(\mu/2t)|$.

Non-Abelian Statistics

Majorana zero modes obey non-Abelian braiding statistics. Exchanging two Majoranas applies a unitary gate to the degenerate ground state space — the foundation of topological quantum computing.

Weyl Semimetals

$$H(\mathbf{k}) = v_F\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}_0)$$

Weyl semimetals are 3D topological phases where conduction and valence bands touch at isolated points (Weyl nodes) in the Brillouin zone. Near each node, the dispersion is linear — a 3D Dirac cone.

Weyl Nodes as Monopoles

$$C_\pm = \pm 1 = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{S^2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$

Each Weyl node carries a Chern number $C = \pm 1$ — it is a monopole of Berry curvature in k-space. Nodes come in pairs of opposite chirality. They can only be annihilated in pairs.

Fermi Arcs

On the surface of a Weyl semimetal, the Fermi surface consists of open arcs connecting the projections of opposite-chirality Weyl nodes. These arcs are the hallmark of the Weyl phase.

Chiral Anomaly

$$\partial_\mu j^\mu_5 = \frac{e^2}{2\pi^2\hbar^2}\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$$

Under parallel electric and magnetic fields, charge is pumped between Weyl nodes of opposite chirality — the chiral anomaly. This leads to negative magnetoresistance, a key experimental signature.

Fractional Quantum Hall Effect

The Laughlin wavefunction describes the ground state at filling fraction $\nu = 1/m$ for odd integer $m$. It is the exact zero-energy eigenstate of the Haldane pseudopotential Hamiltonian.

Fractional Charge

Quasihole excitations carry fractional charge $e^* = e/m$. A quasihole at position $\eta$ is created by inserting a flux tube: $\Psi_{\text{qh}} = \prod_i(z_i-\eta)\Psi_m$.

Anyonic Statistics

$$\theta = \frac{\pi}{m}$$

When one quasihole is braided around another, the wavefunction acquires a phase $\theta = \pi/m$. For $m=3$, $\theta = \pi/3$ — anyons intermediate between fermions ($\pi$) and bosons ($0$).

Topological Order

The FQHE exhibits topological order: ground state degeneracy on a torus ($m$-fold), edge modes described by a chiral Luttinger liquid, and quasiparticles with non-Abelian statistics at $\nu = 5/2$ (Moore-Read Pfaffian).

SSH Chain (Su–Schrieffer–Heeger)

$$H = \sum_n\bigl[t_- c_{A,n}^\dagger c_{B,n} + t_+ c_{B,n}^\dagger c_{A,n+1}+{\rm h.c.}\bigr]$$

Prototypical 1D topological insulator with chiral (sublattice) symmetry. Intra-cell hopping $t_-$ and inter-cell $t_+$ set dimerization $\delta=(t_+-t_-)/(t_++t_-)$.

Winding Number

$$\nu=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\rm BZ}dk\,\partial_k\log\det h(k)\in\mathbb{Z}$$

For the two-band Bloch Hamiltonian $h(k)=\mathbf{d}(k)\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, $\nu$ counts how many times $\mathbf{d}(k)$ winds about the origin. $\nu=1$ when $|t_+|>|t_-|$ (topological); $\nu=0$ otherwise.

Zak Phase

$$\gamma_Z=i\int_{\rm BZ}\langle u_k|\partial_k u_k\rangle\,dk=\pi\nu \pmod{2\pi}$$

Bulk–boundary: open chains host exponentially localized zero-energy end modes when $\nu=1$, protected by chiral symmetry (cannot hybridize without gap closing).

Frontier

SSH is the parent of topological pumps (Thouless), photonic/acoustic analogs, and cold-atom implementations of the Zak phase (Atala et al.). Higher-dimensional generalizations feed BBH HOTIs.

Haldane Model on Graphene

$$H=t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}c_i^\dagger c_j + t_2\sum_{\langle\langle ij\rangle\rangle}e^{i\nu_{ij}\phi}c_i^\dagger c_j + M\sum_i\xi_i c_i^\dagger c_i$$

First lattice Chern insulator without net magnetic flux (Haldane 1988). Complex next-nearest-neighbor hopping $t_2 e^{\pm i\phi}$ breaks time-reversal while preserving honeycomb lattice symmetry; staggered mass $M$ breaks inversion.

Dirac Masses & Chern

$$\Delta_{K/K'}=M\pm 3\sqrt{3}\,t_2\sin\phi$$
$$C=\tfrac12\bigl[\mathrm{sgn}\Delta_K-\mathrm{sgn}\Delta_{K'}\bigr]\in\{-1,0,+1\}$$

Gap closings at Dirac points when $\Delta_K=0$ or $\Delta_{K'}=0$ mark topological transitions. $C=\pm1$ yields one chiral edge mode.

Frontier

Cold-atom Floquet realization (Jotzu et al. 2014); graphene/hBN moiré Chern bands; anomalous Hall in magnetic topological insulators. Seed for Kane–Mele (spinful TR-invariant cousin).

Floquet Topological Insulators

$$H(t+T)=H(t),\quad U(T)=e^{-iH_F T/\hbar}$$

Periodic drives generate a Floquet Hamiltonian $H_F$ with quasienergy $\varepsilon\in[-\hbar\omega/2,\hbar\omega/2]$. Topology lives in both 0- and π-gaps of the quasienergy Brillouin zone.

Anomalous Edge Modes

$$C_0,\;C_\pi\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{(gap invariants)}$$

Unlike static systems, Floquet systems can host chiral edge modes in the π-gap even when the static Chern numbers of bulk bands vanish ($C_0=0$, $C_\pi\neq0$) — the anomalous Floquet insulator (Rudner et al.).

Light-Driven Graphene

Circularly polarized light opens a Haldane-type gap at Dirac points via Floquet engineering (Oka–Aoki, Kitagawa). Observed in graphene ARPES and photonic lattices.

Frontier

Prethermalization, micromotion heating, Floquet-Andreev states, discrete time crystals, and topological pumps under continuous drive. Open problem: robust many-body Floquet topology at infinite temperature.

Axion Insulator & Magnetoelectric Effect

$$\mathcal{L}_\theta=\frac{\theta e^2}{4\pi^2\hbar}\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$$

3D topological insulators are described by an effective axion angle $\theta=\pi$ (mod $2\pi$) protected by time-reversal. Breaking TR on surfaces while keeping bulk $\theta=\pi$ yields an axion insulator.

Quantized Magnetoelectric Response

$$\alpha_{\rm ME}=\frac{\theta e^2}{2\pi h}=\frac{e^2}{2h}\quad(\theta=\pi)$$

Each gapped surface contributes half-quantized Hall conductivity $\sigma_{xy}=\pm e^2/2h$. Opposite surfaces cancel net Hall while the bulk magnetoelectric polarizability remains quantized.

Materials

MnBi₂Te₄ even septuple layers; heterostructures with magnetic doping (Cr/V:Bi₂Se₃). Signatures: zero Hall plateau with finite longitudinal resistance, quantized Faraday/Kerr rotation.

Frontier

Dynamical axion fields, axionic polaritons, topological magnetoelectric transistors, and dark-matter axion detectors using TI magnetoelectric response.

Higher-Order Topological Insulators

$$H_{\rm BBH}=\sum_{\rm plaq}\bigl[\gamma+\lambda\cos k\bigr]\text{ hoppings (Benalcazar–Bernevig–Hughes)}$$

An $n$-th order TI in $d$ dimensions hosts gapless modes on $(d-n)$-dimensional boundaries. 2D quadrupole insulator: gapped edges, protected corner zero modes.

Nested Wilson Loops

$$q_{xy}=2p_x^{v_y}p_y^{v_x}\pmod{1},\quad p=\tfrac{1}{2}\text{ (topological)}$$

Bulk multipole moments diagnosed by nested Wilson loops of Wannier bands. Edge polarizations $p=\tfrac12$ force corner charges $Q_{\rm corner}=e/2$ (mod $e$).

3D HOTIs

Chiral/helical hinge modes in bismuth, SnTe, and Bi₄Br₄. Second-order 3D: gapped surfaces, 1D hinges; third-order: corner states only.

Frontier

Fragile topology, boundary-obstructed phases, non-Hermitian HOTIs, photonic/acoustic metamaterial corner lasers, and interacting multipole topology.

Twisted Bilayer Graphene (Moiré Topology Lite)

$$\mathbf{a}_M\sim\frac{a}{2\sin(\theta/2)},\quad\theta_{\rm magic}\approx1.05^\circ\text{–}1.1^\circ$$

Two graphene sheets at relative twist θ form a long-period moiré lattice. Near magic angle, kinetic energy quenches and flat bands emerge (Bistritzer–MacDonald continuum model).

Topology of Flat Bands

$$C=\pm 1\text{ per valley (with alignment / hBN / B)}$$

Each valley-spin sector can carry Chern number $\pm1$. At integer fillings, interactions open Chern insulator gaps (quantum anomalous Hall in hBN-aligned TBG at $\nu=+3$).

Correlated Phases

Mott-like insulators at $\nu=\pm2,\pm3$; unconventional superconductivity near $\nu=-2$; cascade of flavor polarizations. Hofstadter spectrum under magnetic field yields fractal Chern mosaic.

Frontier

Heavy-fermion dual descriptions, skyrmion SC mechanisms, multitwist multilayers (TTG, helical trilayer), and topological quantum criticality in moiré continuum models.

Kitaev Honeycomb Spin Liquid (Lite)

$$H=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle_\alpha}J_\alpha\,\sigma_i^\alpha\sigma_j^\alpha$$

Exactly solvable spin-1/2 model on the honeycomb lattice with bond-directional Ising couplings. Maps to free Majorana fermions coupled to a static $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge field (Kitaev 2006).

Flux Sector & Spectrum

$$W_p=\prod_{\rm plaq}\sigma^\alpha= \pm 1,\quad\text{ground state: all }W_p=+1$$

Isotropic $J_x=J_y=J_z$: gapless Majorana cone (phase B). Anisotropic couplings open a gap (phase A, abelian anyons). Magnetic field $\kappa$ gaps phase B into a non-Abelian Ising topological order with Chern number $C=\pm1$ for Majoranas.

Anyons

Abelian phase: $e$ (vison), $m$, fermion $\varepsilon=e\times m$. Non-Abelian: Ising anyons $\sigma$ (vortex binding Majorana zero mode) with fusion $\sigma\times\sigma=1+\varepsilon$.
