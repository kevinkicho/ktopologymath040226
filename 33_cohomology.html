de Rham's Theorem

H^k_dR(M) ≅ H^k(M; ℝ)

Betti numbers bₖ = dim Hᵏ count k-dimensional holes:

b₀ = connected components
b₁ = independent loops (handles)
b₂ = independent 2-cavities
χ = b₀ − b₁ + b₂ (Euler char.)
Gauss-Bonnet: ∫_M K dA = 2πχ(M) — curvature integrates to topology!
Hodge theorem: every cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative.

The Euler characteristic χ is a topological invariant — it doesn't change under continuous deformation.

Chern Classes

For a complex vector bundle E over M:

c₁(E) = [Tr F/2πi] ∈ H²(M,ℤ)
c₂(E) = [(TrF)²−(TrF²)]/8π²
det(1+itF/2π) = Σ cₖ(E) tᵏ

Chern-Weil theorem: invariant polynomials of curvature give integral cohomology classes.

Physical Applications

c₁ = magnetic monopole charge (Dirac). The Dirac string is invisible iff c₁ ∈ ℤ.
c₂ = Yang-Mills instanton number. Tunneling between vacua in QCD.
Atiyah-Singer: index(D) = ∫ ch(E)·Â(TM) — analytic index = topological index.
Anomaly cancellation in string theory requires c₂(E) = c₂(TM). Green-Schwarz mechanism.

de Rham Complex

Ω⁰ →^d Ω¹ →^d Ω² →^d Ω³
d² = 0 (d∘d = 0)
H^k_dR = ker(d:Ωᵏ→Ωᵏ⁺¹) / im(d:Ωᵏ⁻¹→Ωᵏ)

Closed forms: dω=0 (kernel). Exact forms: ω=dη (image). Cohomology = closed mod exact.

S¹: H⁰=ℝ, H¹=ℝ. The generator of H¹ is dθ — closed (d(dθ)=0) but not exact (θ is multivalued).
T²: H⁰=ℝ, H¹=ℝ², H²=ℝ. Two independent 1-forms: dθ₁ and dθ₂.
S²: H⁰=ℝ, H¹=0, H²=ℝ. No closed non-exact 1-forms — simply connected!

Key Principle

d² = 0 ⟹ im ⊂ ker

This is why cohomology is well-defined: every exact form is automatically closed.

de Rham's theorem: H^k_dR(M) ≅ H^k_singular(M; ℝ) — smooth and topological cohomology agree!

Čech Cohomology

Given a good cover {Uᵢ} of X, the Čech complex uses intersections:

Č⁰ = ∏ Uᵢ   Č¹ = ∏ Uᵢ∩Uⱼ   Č² = ∏ Uᵢ∩Uⱼ∩Uₖ
Ȟᵏ(X, ℤ) = Hᵏ(Č•)
Good cover: all intersections are contractible. Then Čech cohomology equals singular cohomology (Leray's theorem).

Nerve of a Cover

The nerve N(U) has one vertex per Uᵢ, one edge per Uᵢ∩Uⱼ≠∅, one triangle per Uᵢ∩Uⱼ∩Uₖ≠∅, etc.

S¹ with 3 sets: nerve = triangle boundary. H⁰=ℤ (connected), H¹=ℤ (one 1-cycle).
Add 4th set making triple overlap nonempty: nerve gains a filled triangle → becomes contractible → H¹=0!

Čech–de Rham Isomorphism

Ȟᵏ(M, ℝ) ≅ H^k_dR(M)

On smooth manifolds, Čech and de Rham cohomologies agree — two complementary views of the same topology.

Poincaré Duality

For a closed orientable n-manifold M:

Hᵏ(M) ≅ Hⁿ⁻ᵏ(M)
⟨[α],[β]⟩ = ∫_M α∧β

The wedge product and integration pair k-forms with (n-k)-forms non-degenerately.

Torus T² (n=2): H¹(T²)=ℝ². The pairing H¹×H¹→ℝ via ∫dθ₁∧dθ₂ = 1 is the intersection form.
Geometric picture: a 1-cycle γ and 1-cocycle ω — the integral ∫_γ ω counts how many times γ crosses the Poincaré dual of ω.

Poincaré Dual

PD([N]) = [ω_N] ∈ Hⁿ⁻ᵏ(M)

A k-dimensional submanifold N ↔ an (n−k)-cohomology class: ∫_N α = ∫_M α∧ω_N.

For the torus: the horizontal circle gives a class in H₁, its Poincaré dual is a 1-form whose integral across any transverse curve = intersection number.

bₖ = bₙ₋ₖ (Poincaré duality)

T²: b₀=b₂=1, b₁=2. The middle dimension b₁=2 reflects two independent loops.

Characteristic Classes

For a complex line bundle L (U(1) bundle) over M:

c₁(L) = [F/2π] ∈ H²(M, ℤ)

The first Chern class is the integral cohomology class of the curvature 2-form divided by 2π.

Integer obstruction: c₁ ∈ ℤ measures how twisted the bundle is. c₁=0 iff the bundle is trivial (globally).

Classifying Space

BU(1) = ℂP^∞
[M, ℂP^∞] ≅ H²(M, ℤ)

Any U(1) bundle over M is classified by a map M→ℂP^∞. The generator of H²(ℂP^∞)=ℤ pulls back to c₁.

ℂP¹ = S²: the Hopf fibration S³→S² is the non-trivial U(1) bundle with c₁=1.

Euler Class

e(E) ∈ Hⁿ(M) for rank-n bundle

The Euler class of the tangent bundle equals the Euler characteristic: ∫_M e(TM) = χ(M).

Gauss-Bonnet: ∫_M K dA = 2πχ(M). This is Euler class = Chern-Weil representative of e(TM).
Physical: c₁ counts vortex lines (Abrikosov vortices in superconductors) or magnetic monopoles. Each vortex contributes one flux quantum Φ₀ = h/e.

Sheaf Cohomology

A sheaf ℱ on X assigns groups ℱ(U) to open sets, with restriction maps. The cohomology Hᵖ(X, ℱ) measures global obstructions to patching local sections.

0 → H⁰(X,ℱ) → ∏ ℱ(Uᵢ) → ∏ ℱ(Uᵢ∩Uⱼ) → …
H⁰ = global sections (functions that agree everywhere). Higher Hᵖ = obstructions to gluing.

GAGA Principle

Hᵖ(X^an, ℱ^an) ≅ Hᵖ(X, ℱ)

Serre (1956): for a projective variety X over ℂ, algebraic and analytic cohomologies agree. Holomorphic = algebraic on compact spaces!

GAGA = Géométrie Algébrique et Géométrie Analytique. Bridging abstract algebra and complex analysis.

Penrose Transform

massless field of helicity s ↔ H¹(PT, O(−2s−2))

Twistor space PT = ℂP³\ℂP¹. Massless free fields on Minkowski space ↔ sheaf cohomology classes on twistor space.

s=1 (Maxwell): electromagnetic field ↔ H¹(PT, O(−4)). s=2 (gravity): graviton ↔ H¹(PT, O(−6)).
The Penrose transform converts PDE solutions into sheaf cohomology — topology encodes physics!
On ℂP¹: Hᵖ(ℂP¹, O(n)) = 0 for p=1, n≥−1. Riemann-Roch: χ(O(n)) = n+1 (for n≥0).

Singular & Simplicial Cohomology

A singular k-simplex is a continuous map σ: Δᵏ → X. Chains Cₖ(X) are formal R-linear combinations of these; the boundary ∂: Cₖ→Cₖ₋₁ satisfies ∂²=0.

Cᵏ(X;R) := Hom(Cₖ(X), R)
δφ = φ ∘ ∂   (coboundary)
Hᵏ(X;R) = ker δ / im δ
Simplicial cohomology uses the ordered simplices of a Δ-complex (or simplicial set). For good triangulations it agrees with singular cohomology.

Mayer–Vietoris Sequence

If X = U ∪ V with U,V open (or more generally a good excisive triad), there is a long exact sequence:

… → Hᵏ(X) → Hᵏ(U)⊕Hᵏ(V) → Hᵏ(U∩V) → Hᵏ⁺¹(X) → …
δ: Hᵏ(U∩V) → Hᵏ⁺¹(X)   (connecting)
Geometric δ: a cocycle on U∩V that is a coboundary on U and on V glues to a cocycle on X after a degree shift — the obstruction to global extension.

Spectral Sequences (Leray–Serre)

A spectral sequence is a book of pages (E_r, d_r) with d_r: E_r^{p,q} → E_r^{p+r,q−r+1}, d_r²=0, and E_{r+1} = H(E_r,d_r). It “converges” to a graded object associated to a filtration of the target.

E₂^{p,q} = Hᵖ(B; ℋᵍ(F)) ⇒ H^{p+q}(E)
d_r : E_r^{p,q} → E_r^{p+r, q−r+1}
Leray: for a map f:X→Y and sheaf ℱ, E₂^{p,q}=Hᵖ(Y; Rᵍf_*ℱ) ⇒ H^{p+q}(X;ℱ). Serre SS is the fibration case with constant coefficients (local systems if π₁ acts).

Cup Product & Cohomology Ring

(φ ⌣ ψ)(σ) = φ(σ|[v₀…vₚ]) · ψ(σ|[vₚ…v_{p+q}])
α ⌣ β = (−1)^{|α||β|} β ⌣ α

de Rham model: [α] ⌣ [β] = [α ∧ β]. Poincaré duality pairs via cup then evaluate on fundamental class.

H*(T²) ≅ Λ(x,y) exterior algebra on two degree-1 generators; x⌣y generates H².
H*(ℂPⁿ) ≅ ℤ[c]/(c^{n+1}) with |c|=2 — pure polynomial truncated. Steenrod ops act nontrivially mod p.
Homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic rings — but the ring distinguishes spaces with the same Betti numbers (e.g. T² vs S¹∨S¹∨S²).

Frontier

String topology (Chas–Sullivan): H_*(LM) is a BV algebra. Factorization homology upgrades cup products to E_n structures. Derived algebraic geometry: derived schemes have cotangent complexes whose cohomology is a ringed shadow of the space.

Hodge Theory · Harmonic Forms

On a closed Riemannian manifold, the Hodge Laplacian Δ = dδ + δd (δ = −*d* codifferential) yields the orthogonal decomposition:

Ωᵏ = ℋᵏ ⊕ im d ⊕ im δ
Hᵏ_dR(M) ≅ ℋᵏ = ker Δ ∩ Ωᵏ
Heat flow e^{−tΔ}ω → harmonic projection as t→∞. Analytic index of elliptic complexes = topological index (Atiyah–Singer).

Kähler / Dolbeault

Hᵏ_dR(X;ℂ) ≅ ⊕_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}_∂̄(X)

On compact Kähler manifolds the Hodge decomposition is pure; Hodge numbers h^{p,q}=dim H^{p,q} refine Betti numbers.

Bridge from Ch.16: exterior calculus + metric → * operator → Δ. Cohomology becomes linear algebra of harmonics.
Frontier: non-abelian Hodge (Higgs bundles ↔ local systems), mixed Hodge structures on singular varieties (Deligne), p-adic Hodge theory (Fontaine).

Equivariant Cohomology (lite)

For a G-space X, ordinary H*(X/G) fails when action is not free. Borel construction replaces X/G by homotopy quotient:

H*_G(X) := H*(EG ×_G X)
H*_G(pt) = H*(BG)
Cartan model (G compact connected): (S(g*)⊗Ω(X))^G with d_G = d − ι_ξ·u^ξ. Equivariant forms encode moment maps in symplectic geometry.

Localization (ABBV)

∫_X α = Σ_F ∫_F (α|_F / e(ν_F))

Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne: equivariant integrals reduce to fixed-point data. Enormous power in enumerative geometry and supersymmetric localization in QFT.

H*_T(pt) ≅ ℤ[u₁,…,u_r] for torus T. Module structure over H*_G(pt) is the key algebraic package.
Frontier: equivariant elliptic cohomology, Borel vs Bredon vs RO(G)-graded theories for finite groups; Gromov–Witten via virtual localization.
